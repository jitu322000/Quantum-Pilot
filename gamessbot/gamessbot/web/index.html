Common mistakes & best practices

  • Read the suggested active space's orbital character before accepting it, an orbital sitting right at the SAP coefficient threshold may be a core/Rydberg-like orbital that doesn't belong in the active space, or a genuinely important one that only just cleared the cutoff, either way it's worth a look, not a rubber stamp.
  • If CASSCF doesn't converge at MAXIT=120/200, don't just keep raising MAXIT, a near-degenerate or poorly-chosen active space often won't converge no matter how many iterations you give it. The smaller-active-space-then-regrow recovery (or manually picking a smaller, better-behaved active space first) usually gets further than brute-forcing iterations.
  • State-average over exactly as many states as you actually care about (NSTATE), including extra states you don't need pulls the CASSCF orbitals toward describing states you won't use, which can quietly worsen convergence and accuracy for the ones you do want.
  • Starting a new active-space/state combination from a previous combination's optimized (possibly MO-reordered) orbitals can help convergence when the two active spaces are closely related, but for an unrelated or much larger active space it can just as easily carry over an unhelpful orbital ordering, when in doubt, start fresh from the closed-shell orbitals.
  • XMCQDPT is far more expensive than the CASSCF it builds on, and TRANSITN reruns much of the CASSCF setup again to compute transition moments, only turn these on for the active-space/state combinations you're actually going to report, not every exploratory combination.
  • Bigger active spaces and larger basis sets both grow cost fast (CASSCF scales combinatorially with active orbitals; XMCQDPT/TRANSITN add on top of that), it's usually more productive to first nail down a good, chemically-motivated active space, then move to a larger basis set once you're confident in it, rather than maximizing both at once.
  • Oscillator strengths from TRANSITN are relative to S0 by construction, if you need transitions between two excited states rather than from the ground state, this tool's TRANSITN output won't give you that directly.
  • Right-size the memory (MWORDS) to the active space and basis set, too little causes GAMESS to fail outright on a multireference job; padding it far beyond what's needed just wastes memory without speeding anything up.
